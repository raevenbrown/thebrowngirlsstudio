The AI Lab · InspiHER, Dec 2-3, 2026

Run of show: four sessions, four different missions

Each session is 60 minutes, 20 girls, Chromebooks, Wi-Fi on site, a projector or Cleartouch panel (not confirmed which). Every one has a different main activity so no two rooms repeat. Room and times still to be confirmed with Dr. Bowden.

Read this first

About teaching girls to set up Claude or other AI accounts. Claude's consumer app is for adults 18 and older, so under-18 girls cannot create their own Claude accounts. Other tools set their own age rules and many need a parent. Newton also blocks most AI tools on student Chromebooks (Dr. Bowden, Nov 16). So at InspiHER the girls do the hands-on work in your class web page with a class code, and you run the live AI demos on your laptop. Check each tool's current age terms before you promise anything to parents.

What you can offer parents instead: a short "AI at home" talk or handout for adults on how to set up an adult account safely, turn on privacy settings, and sit with their daughter while she explores. That is a good Season 2 parent night.

The four sessions

Wed Dec 2 · 5th grade · 20 girls

Session 1: AI Detectives

Mission: Learn that AI is a tool that learns from examples, and practice spotting what it gets right and wrong.

What is different from the other sessions: Detective game and a unplugged 'robot' activity. No typing prompts yet.

0-5Welcome and hook
Ask: what do you think AI is? Hands up for 'I used it', 'I saw it', 'never heard of it'. Say the three rules: be kind, no personal info, ask when unsure.
5-20Be the robot
Unplugged. Girls sort picture cards by rules one pair gives them. One girl is the 'AI', her partner is the 'trainer'. Then swap. Debrief: what happens when the examples are bad?
20-45Detective round
On the class web page: guess AI or human, then build an 'AI helper card' (its name, its one job, one thing it must never do).
45-50Gallery walk
Helper cards on screens. Girls leave a gold star on two they like.
50-60Share, feedback, QR
Two girls show their work. Anonymous feedback on the Chromebook (no names, no emails). Hold up the QR card only if the district approved it.

Bring

  • Laptop, USB-C and HDMI adapters
  • Card deck for the robot activity (24 cards, 2 sets)
  • Printed class codes
  • Gold star stickers

If tech fails

  • Run the robot activity for 25 minutes and skip the web page
  • Cards plus a paper helper-card template
Wed Dec 2 · 5th grade · 20 girls

Session 2: Teach a Robot Pet

Mission: Understand how training examples shape what a machine learns, then design a pet with rules.

What is different from the other sessions: Different from Session 1: the main build is a 'robot pet' with rules and a show-and-tell voting round, not detective work.

0-5Welcome and hook
Ask: what do you think AI is? Hands up for 'I used it', 'I saw it', 'never heard of it'. Say the three rules: be kind, no personal info, ask when unsure.
5-20Teach a classmate
One girl pretends to be a robot pet. The group gives her rules, one at a time. Watch what breaks when a rule is unclear. Laugh. Debrief.
20-45Design your pet
Web page: choose pet type, write three rules, test it against three 'tricky' situations. Fix one rule.
45-50Pet parade
Each table shows one pet and the rule they had to fix. Group votes on 'most helpful pet'.
50-60Share, feedback, QR
Two girls show their work. Anonymous feedback on the Chromebook (no names, no emails). Hold up the QR card only if the district approved it.

Bring

  • Laptop and adapters
  • Timer on screen
  • Sticky notes for rule ideas
  • Printed class codes

If tech fails

  • Paper pet-design sheet and the unplugged game only
  • Have girls act out the tricky situations
Thu Dec 3 · grades 6-8 · 20 girls

Session 3: Prompt Lab

Mission: Learn that clear instructions get better results, and practice checking what AI says.

What is different from the other sessions: More writing and testing than the 5th grade sessions. Girls improve prompts and fact-check one answer.

0-5Welcome and hook
Ask: what do you think AI is? Hands up for 'I used it', 'I saw it', 'never heard of it'. Say the three rules: be kind, no personal info, ask when unsure.
5-15Same ask, different answer
On your laptop, adult-led: ask one vague prompt and one clear prompt. Show the difference on the projector. Introduce the four parts: role, task, details, format.
15-40Prompt challenge
Teams get a mission on the class web page, such as plan a study week or explain a topic to a 5th grader. They write, test and improve three versions.
40-50Catch the mistake
Show one AI answer with a planted error. Teams find it and say how they would check it.
50-60Share, feedback, QR
Two girls show their work. Anonymous feedback on the Chromebook (no names, no emails). Hold up the QR card only if the district approved it.

Bring

  • Laptop with a browser logged in by you
  • Adapters, hotspot as backup
  • Printed four-part prompt cards
  • Planted-error handout

If tech fails

  • Show saved screenshots of the two answers
  • Teams write prompts on paper and you read them aloud
Thu Dec 3 · grades 9-12 · 20 girls

Session 4: Future Builder

Mission: Connect AI to real careers and college, build an idea pitch, and talk about using AI honestly.

What is different from the other sessions: Career and ethics focus. Girls pitch an AI idea and sign an honest-use pledge. This is the session for the adult-led Claude demo.

0-5Welcome and hook
Ask: what do you think AI is? Hands up for 'I used it', 'I saw it', 'never heard of it'. Say the three rules: be kind, no personal info, ask when unsure.
5-15What jobs does AI touch?
Quick poll: pick a career. Table groups list what AI can help with and what it should never do for that job.
15-30Adult-led demo on the projector
Three short demos from you: turn a messy idea into a weekly plan, explain one idea three ways, then ask for sources and check one live. Girls call out what to ask next.
30-48Pitch your idea
Teams of four write a 60-second pitch for an AI helper that solves a real problem for girls their age. Two pitches go live.
48-50Honest-use pledge
Each girl writes one rule she will follow when she uses AI for school.
50-60Share, feedback, QR
Two girls show their work. Anonymous feedback on the Chromebook (no names, no emails). Hold up the QR card only if the district approved it.

Bring

  • Laptop logged in by you (adult account)
  • Adapters and hotspot
  • Pitch template and honest-use pledge cards
  • Phone or tablet to show QR if needed

If tech fails

  • Run the demos from saved screenshots
  • Pitches on paper, read aloud

Do this every session (5 minutes before the girls arrive)

  • Test the projector or panel with your adapters. Connect your hotspot as backup.
  • Put the class code on the board in big letters.
  • Open the student page on one Chromebook to check it loads.
  • Put the QR card by the door only if the district approved it.
